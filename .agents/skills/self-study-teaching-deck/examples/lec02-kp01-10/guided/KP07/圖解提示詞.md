Like archery: the loss is the distance of the arrow from the bullseye. MSE squares it, so a wild arrow is heavily punished; MAE uses the distance itself and is more tolerant; Huber acts like MSE up close and like MAE far away. Classification measures not a distance but how far the predicted probability is from the correct answer.

MSE $\sum e^2$; MAE $\sum|e|$; Huber uses $\tfrac12e^2$ when $|e|\le\delta$ and $\delta(|e|-\tfrac12\delta)$ otherwise; cross-entropy $-\sum\sum y_l\log f_l$; KL $\sum\sum y_l\log(y_l/f_l)$; hinge $\max(0,m-yf_o)$.
